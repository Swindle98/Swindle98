METAL ORGANIC FRAMEWORKS (MOFS) AS A CARBON CAPTURE SYSTEM FOR CYANOBACTERIA AND ALGAE,

OCTOBER 2022 – DECEMBER 2022 BBSRC DTP ROTATION PROJECT, DR ANDREA LAYBOURN, UNIVERSITY OF NOTTINGHAM A pilot study into the feasibility of using MOFs to selectively capture CO2 from industrial source. The captured CO2 could be passed to cyanobacteria, converting the waste gas into valuable byproducts. The research undertaken during this research project focused on the cytotoxicity of the MOFs should they end up on solution with the cyanobacteria.

Techniques used included; cyanobacteria/cell culturing, oxygen evolution quantification, microwave MOF synthesis, scanning electron microscopy(SEM), thermos-gravimetric analysis (TGA), X-ray diffraction.

GLIDEOSOME INVASION PROTIENS IN PLASMODIUM BERGHEI,

SEPTEMBER 2019 – MARCH 2020 4TH YEAR PROJECT, TEWARI LAB (PROF. RITA TEWARI), UNIVERSITY OF NOTTINGHAM A study into the role of glideosome proteins across the life cycle of P. berghei, their role in host invasion, and sexual reproduction of the parasite. Initial research on the topic was presented as a poster with findings from the year presented as a dissertation in the form of an academic paper and defended during a viva voce.

The wet lab skills involved: PCR, western blots, protein pulldown, molecular cloning techniques, GFP tagging proteins, and immunofluorescence imaging. The project also involved a brief delve into proteomics, studying the interactome of the target protein.

Beyond the practical and analytical techniques that went into the research and write up, this project served as an introduction into academic lab community providing an invaluable understanding of lab dynamics, procedures, how science actually gets done and where the lab sits within the institution as a whole.

COMPARISONS OF PANC1 CELLS TO PANCREATIC CANCER CELL SAMPLES,

SPRING 2019 3RD YEAR PROJECT, DR PAUL SCOTTING & DR PADDY TIGHE, UNIVERSITY OF NOTTINGHAM An in silico study using publicly available transcriptome data from negative controls using the Panc1 cell line (derived from a pancreatic section), then comparing this to transcriptome data from pancreatic tissue sections. Differences and similarities were highlighted, to determine the accuracy of Panc1 cells as models to study pancreatic cancer.

This project introduced a first glimpse of “independent” bioinformatics (away from the set quizzes of university workshops), it laid a foundational knowledge of online tools (DAVID, NCBI GEO, ETC), microarray specific tools, (J-express, MEV, RMA) culminating in a dissertation and an online passport for our cell line documenting important information and research breakthroughs attained using Panc1.

POSTER: BACTRIOPHAGE RESISTANCE TO ANTIBIOTIC RESISTANT ACINOBACTER BAUMANII

AUTUMN 2018 3RD YEAR PATHOGENS PROJECT, PROF. LIZ SOCKETT, UNIVERSITY OF NOTTINGHAM A mock poster conference hosted as part of a pathogens module, focusing on the successful use of bacteriophage to cure a patient suffering from a terminal A. baumanii in America.

LIVING ALTERNATIVES TO CONVENTIONAL ANTIBIOTICS
